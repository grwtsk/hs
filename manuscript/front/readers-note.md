# A place in which an account can change

Agent-authored provisional reader's note for issue #16, proposed under #6.
This is not accepted author prose, an approved definition of Hs, or a completed
front-matter commission. Source direction: [NAV-01](../../sources/conversation/03-edit-navigation.md),
[the commission](https://github.com/grwtsk/hs/issues/1), and
[the rigor instruction](https://github.com/grwtsk/hs/issues/74).

A house offers more than a place to enter. It also has to leave room for what
happens after arrival: a question that becomes more difficult, an account that
needs correcting, a person who no longer wishes to speak. Hs carries the author's
meanings Haas, Hospital and House: sanctuary, care and communion. These meanings
set a demand on the work. Shelter that is available only while an account remains
agreeable has made agreement its price. An account admitted without any way to
question it has made shelter into possession of the conclusion.

The proposed alternative is not a compromise in which truth receives less care
and the person receives less freedom. It begins by keeping two questions apart.
What has the person chosen to express? What grounds support a particular claim?
The first can be honored without deciding the second in advance. The second can
be examined without taking away the person's place in the encounter.

NAV-01 gives this distinction a concrete setting. The page is already the input.
A person editing a link has expressed something; they should not have to translate
that act into a second intensity setting before it is received. Yet changing the
link's words does not change another author's source. Nor does typing a destination
make protected material available. A useful assistant can explain that difference
once, intelligibly, while preserving the permitted edit and route. Resistance
becomes accountable when its reason can be examined and dismissed, not when it
makes the person win a contest to proceed.

Consider a wholly synthetic example. Two contributors identify an apple tree.
One offers a dated observation; the other supplies a shared map. Their accounts
may refer to the same tree, but calling it *the apple tree* does not establish
that identity. The record must carry the relation that makes the definite phrase
usable. A person may follow one permitted route while the competing identification
remains visible. The choice of route neither proves equivalence nor requires
pretending the two accounts have equal grounds.

An objection arises here. If every question remains available, does the house
become a place where nothing can be decided? That would be another failure of
care. An established date need not be suspended because a motive is unknown.
A corrected identification can justify changing the affected sentence. What must
remain open is the route by which a judgment can be examined, not every conclusion
indefinitely. A correction should change precisely what its grounds require;
it should not become permission to erase all earlier testimony or to preserve
an error because it once belonged to someone.

The rigor instruction names racism as a case that must remain examinable. In a
second, wholly synthetic case, a hearing's visible evidence schedule lists one
received account but not another. Relevant supplied identities and the scope of
that schedule must remain attributable. Unknown motive does not erase the
supported difference. It also does not supply a racial motive. An unsolicited
assurance that no one has been proved racist answers a different question if it
replaces the inquiry into selection. Description itself must answer for why it
selected these facts, which alternatives it omitted, and what further evidence
would change the account. Institutional assurances, denials and this note's own
formulations receive that scrutiny too. Equal rigor does not promise equal
credibility or manufacture an opposing claim.

Courage in this setting is sustained accountable inquiry. Sometimes it requires
a warranted judgment rather than another postponement. Sometimes it requires
recognizing that one's preferred account has failed. Sometimes it means keeping
one specific unknown in view without scattering doubt over everything known.
None of these acts requires aggression or compulsory personal disclosure.
Gentleness concerns how the encounter is made possible; it is not a license to
weaken the work once the person has arrived.

These distinctions have consequences for the book as well as for a future
interface. A source must not be rewritten to make it anticipate a later correction.
A draft must not masquerade as the author's accepted philosophy. A reader needs
to see where an argument stands, what it proposes, and what has not been decided.
The house cannot guarantee that every account will survive unchanged. It can be
answerable for whether the person, the grounds and the possibility of correction
remain present together.
